# Lighthouse Lights and Foggy Whispers

**Thornday, Thaw 11, Year 1 – 05:50**

---

### The Day the Lamp Awoke

The lighthouse at the edge of the harbor flared to life at high noon, a bright beacon cutting through a sky that was otherwise clear. *Ines Marlowe*, keeper of the lantern, recalled the moment with a quiet awe:

> "I checked the switch twice. It was off, yet the lamp burned as if the sea herself had turned the wick."

*Emmet Sallow* swears he saw a distant light answer that flare two nights ago, a claim that has stirred the town’s imagination:

> "Two nights ago a light answered the lighthouse from far out. Nobody believes me. Ines might."

The coincidence of the lamp’s daylight pulse with an unusually high tide has left many wondering if the sea is speaking directly to Vesper.

---

### Fog, Bells, and a Stalled Clock

At ten‑o’clock the pier vanished beneath a thick, rolling fog that seemed to swallow wood and stone alike. *Juno Farrow* watched the scene from the *Salt Kettle* and muttered:

> "The fog rolled in so thick the pier disappeared. I fear the tide will keep us from the water for days."

Soon after, a phantom bell rang from the empty ferry office, though no ferry has docked in months. *Cassius Ode*, clerk of the office, noted:

> "The bell rang again at quarter past four. I kept my eyes east as always."

Later, the town square clock halted at a quarter past, twice in the same evening, as if time itself were caught in the same mist.

---

### Voices of Concern

*Petra Vell*, town clerk, drafted a notice warning of the high tide and the lingering fog. In a hurried scribble she wrote:

> "The tide today has risen beyond any record and the lighthouse lamp lit itself in daylight, a sign we must not ignore. The fog rolls thick enough to hide the pier; fishermen are to stay ashore until further notice."

*Halcyon Brisk*, the town’s doctor, observed the clustering oddities:

> "The lighthouse lit in daylight, a phantom ferry bell, a veil of fog swallowing the pier, and the square clock frozen – all point to a rhythm we have not yet understood."

*Wren Halloway* worries that *Juno Farrow* may board a ferry that never arrives, fearing the apprentice’s savings are being wasted:

> "I’m restless. Juno will board the first boat that ever arrives. Until the bell truly calls, we must keep her grounded."

---

### Notice Board

- **High Tide Advisory** – All residents are advised to avoid low‑lying docks until the tide recedes. The town hall steps now hold a jar of sea‑glass, a reminder of the sea’s gifts and its warnings.
- **Fog‑Watch** – The pier will remain closed until the fog lifts. Any work on the waterfront must receive clearance from the clerk.
- **Clock Repair** – The square clock will be examined by *Cassius Ode* and *Petra Vell* tomorrow morning. Citizens are asked to report any further time anomalies.

---

### A Letter from the Shore

> *Dear Chronicle,*
>
> *I have watched the lighthouse pulse with the tide for weeks now. Each flash seems to pull at something deep inside me, a memory of a night when the sea answered a distant lantern. If anyone else feels the same, let us meet by the kelp‑rock at dusk. Perhaps together we can hear what the sea is trying to tell us.*
>
> – *Mara Kettle*, net‑mender

---

May the lighthouse guide us, and may the fog soon lift.

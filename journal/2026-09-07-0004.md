# The Vesper Chronicle – Issue 4

*Moonday, Thaw 8, Year 1*

---

## The Lamp that Lit the Day

Ines Marlowe, keeper of the lighthouse, reported a strange occurrence that has the town buzzing like a tide‑pool at low water. "The lamp came on by itself at noon," she reflected, noting she had checked the switch twice and found it firmly off. Her calm tone belied the unease that settled over the square as the daylight flash pierced the thickening fog.

Emmet Sallow, retired fisher, swears he saw a distant light answer the lighthouse two nights ago, a claim met with polite skepticism. "Two nights ago a light answered the lighthouse from far out. Nobody believes me. Ines might," he recalled while sharing a jar of sea‑glass with Petra Vell at the town hall steps.

Sable Tide, the tide‑reader, has taken it upon herself to track who else may have witnessed the mysterious answer. "Strange signs—daylight flashes, a phantom ferry bell, thick fog swallowing the pier, and a jar of sea glass—hint that the lighthouse's flashes are timed to the tide," she wrote in her notebook. Her cheerful demeanor masks a growing curiosity, and she has begun a quiet canvass of the Pier End and the Salt Kettle.

## High Tide, Higher Concerns

The town’s tide has risen beyond any recorded level, a fact echoed in the observations of every citizen who stepped near the water today. Petra Vell, town clerk, notes in her draft notice: "The tide today has risen beyond any record and the lighthouse lamp lit itself in daylight, a sign we must not ignore."

Halcyon Brisk, the town’s doctor, reported a lingering fog that cloaked the pier from 15:00 onward, turning the streets into rivers. "Even after three months I have not unpacked my second bag, and no one has asked why I arrived, reinforcing my wariness of strangers," she thought, yet she added, "I notice the lighthouse lights itself in daylight and flares without cause, and several townsfolk keep remarking on it."

The ferry bell, which rings each quarter past four, has taken on a new, unsettling rhythm. Cassius Ode, ferry clerk, observed, "The ferry bell rang at a quarter past four. There is no ferry; I sense the bell may be warning of something else."

## Conversations at the Salt Kettle

The Salt Kettle café, home to many impromptu meetings, has become the de‑facto briefing room. Juno Farrow, the apprentice, confessed to Wren Halloway, shopkeeper, "those fourteen extra coins still don't cover the ferry, and the tide's rising." Their shared worry—whether the ferry will ever arrive—mirrors the broader unease.

Tobiah Renn, baker, tried a new rye loaf that kept coming out flat. He mused, "I sense the sea is speaking to me, leaving glass jars and keeping my rye flat, as if its tide guides my bake." His reflection intertwined the bakery’s rhythm with the town’s maritime pulse.

## Notices and Calls for Vigilance

- **Town Notice (draft)** – *Prepared by Petra Vell*: "Due to an unusually high tide and unexplained lighthouse flashes, all sea‑bound activities are suspended after 17:00. Citizens are urged to keep to the streets and report any further anomalies to the lighthouse keeper."
- **Call for Witnesses** – *Posted at the Square*: "If you observed a light answering the lighthouse after dark, please report to Ines Marlowe at the lighthouse or to Cassius Ode at the Ferry Office. Your testimony may help decipher this mystery."

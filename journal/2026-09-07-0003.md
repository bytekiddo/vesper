# Lights, Tides, and Sea‑Glass: Vesper’s Whispered Signals

**Saltday, Thaw 6, Year 1** – The town awoke to a sky brushed with the pale gold of early morning, the tide still low enough to see the shimmer of shells on the shore.

---

### The Lighthouse Whispers
> *"The lamp’s odd self‑lighting still feels like a message, so I’ll watch the rocks and wait,"* reflected Ines Marlowe, keeper of the Vesper Lighthouse, after the beacon blazed on its own at noon.  She noted the switch was off, the circuitry untouched, and the light pulsed twice before dimming.  The same phenomenon was echoed by Petra Vell, town clerk, who recorded, *"Today the lighthouse lamp came on by itself in broad daylight. I must ensure the notice is clear and prompt, lest the tide catch us.*"  Both citizens agree the light seems to answer the tide, a theory echoed by tide‑reader Sable Tide: *"I think the lighthouse's flashes sync with the tide, so whatever it signals, the sea hears it.*"

---

### A Jar of Sea‑Glass on the Hall Steps
> *"Someone left a jar of sea glass on the town hall steps,"* murmured Cassius Ode while checking the quarterly reports.  The jar, filled with smooth, sea‑worn pieces, was discovered just after the rain that turned the streets into ribbons of water.  Residents gathered around, turning the glass over in their hands, wondering who had placed the gift.  Emmet Sallow, retired fisher, recalled, *"Two nights ago a light answered the lighthouse from far out. Nobody believes me. Ines might; today someone left a jar of sea glass on the town hall steps.*"  The mystery remains unsolved, but the glass has become a quiet talisman for those watching the shore.

---

### The Bell That Rings Without a Ferry
> *"The ferry bell rang at a quarter past four. There is no ferry; I sense the bell may be warning of something beyond the missing ferry,"* wrote Cassius Ode in his log.  The bell’s clang echoed across the square, stirring the thoughts of Halcyon Brisk, who noted, *"I notice the lighthouse lights itself in daylight and flares without cause, and several townsfolk keep remarking on it.*"  Both the bell and the rogue light have sparked a shared speculation: perhaps they are a coordinated signal, a tide‑coded message meant for those who listen.

---

### Official Notice: Unusually High Tide
Petra Vell, clerk, issued a brief but urgent notice posted on the hall’s bulletin board:

> **Notice – High Tide Alert**
> The tide reached the third step of the town hall today, a level not recorded in the past year. All residents are advised to secure loose items along the pier and keep shutters closed after dusk. The lighthouse’s unexpected illumination may be related to the tide’s behavior.

---

### Letter from the Apprentice
> *From:* Juno Farrow, apprentice at the Salt Kettle
> *To:* The Vesper Chronicle
> *Subject:* A Coin, A Dream
> 
> "I am saving for a ferry ticket that cannot be used. Fifty‑one coins sit in my tin, shy of the sixty I need. The bell rings, the light flashes, and I wonder if the sea will ever carry me away. Until then, I will keep the tin close and listen for any sign the town may share."

---

As the day folds into a gentle dusk, the town’s citizens linger on the pier, eyes turned eastward, ears attuned to the bell, and fingers tracing the smooth sea‑glass. Whether the lighthouse, the tide, or the bell speak a single message remains a question that drifts with the surf, waiting for the next tide to answer.
